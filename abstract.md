# Evaluating the Effectiveness of Multi-Agent Systems Based on Small Language Models Compared to Larger Parameter Models

**Abstract:** The rapid development of large language models has created new opportunities for building intelligent systems capable of reasoning, decision-making, and generating complex textual responses. However, the use of large models  often requires significant computational resources, which may not always be available on local devices. This study explores an alternative approach: the development of a multi-agent system based on a smaller language model. The proposed architecture includes a controller agent responsible for receiving the user prompt, distributing tasks among specialized decision-making agents, collecting their responses, and generating a unified final answer.

The main objective of this research is to evaluate whether multiple agents using the same small model can improve response quality through task decomposition, role specialization, comparison, and synthesis. The study analyzes the potential advantages and limitations of this approach in relation to larger single-model systems. While a multi-agent structure does not increase the fundamental knowledge capacity of the base model, it may improve the reasoning process by forcing the system to examine the prompt from different perspectives. This can be useful for academic writing, brainstorming, problem analysis, decision support, and structured response generation.

Such systems remain limited by the capabilities of the underlying small language model, especially in tasks requiring deep domain knowledge, complex reasoning, or advanced code generation. The expected result is that a well-designed multi-agent architecture can outperform a single small model and may approach the quality of some medium-sized models, but it is unlikely to consistently surpass strong models. The research contributes to understanding how and when small language models can be used more effectively in resource-constrained environments.

**Keywords:** Multi-agent system; small language models; artificial intelligence; prompt distribution; reasoning agents; local AI deployment.
